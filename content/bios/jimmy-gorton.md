Improvement is supposed to be hard-won and uneven. Nobody told Jimmy Gorton. Across four seasons his winners per game have simply gone up and up — 3.0, 4.5, 4.4, 5.25 — while his error rate has barely moved, the kind of curve coaches frame and put on the wall. The results have tracked accordingly: 50% win rates in his first two seasons gave way to 75% in Season 3 (and a top-five MVP finish) and 62.5% in Season 4, lifting his career mark to 61% — fourth-best among the league's long-serving regulars. Twenty-eight matches, 122 winners and six best-on-court awards into his career, Gorton has become the model of the self-made Tuesday night player: no single outrageous weapon, just a game that gets measurably better every single season. On current trajectory, a season MVP feels less like a hope than a schedule.
